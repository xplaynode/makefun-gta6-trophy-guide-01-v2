# Tournament — how this entry's shape was chosen

Competition: gta6-trophy-guide-01. Judged on the kit rubric, in order:
(1) tracker and its data, (2) roadmap and missables built from the official
descriptions, (3) cited guidance in our own words, (4) fast, clear, polished,
small first load. Every candidate below was scored pairwise against those four
criteria; a candidate only advanced by beating another on the earliest criterion
where they differed, unless a later criterion differed by much more.

## Sources read before building (20 Nov 2026)

[1] PowerPyx — GTA 6 Trophy Guide & Roadmap (full page read: roadmap, all 55
per-trophy notes, meditation, strangers, robberies). [2] OutputLag — GTA 6
Trophy List (full list, grades, Gamerscore, missable note). [3] KeenGamer —
GTA 6 Trophy Guide & Platinum Roadmap (roadmap, missable section, collectible
table). [4] eGamers (egamers.io) — GTA 6 Trophy List plus its 100% completion
companion (grade split 27 visible / 28 hidden, skip thresholds). [5] Exophase —
GTA VI trophy list (tracker listing; the list the kit's starter was checked
against). [6] PSNProfiles — GTA VI trophy list (tracker listing; PSN points
total 1,350 reported for the set). [7] GTABase — GTA 6 Trophy & Achievements
list (full list, roadmap notes, character quest lines). [8] Push Square — GTA 6
Trophy Guide & Roadmap plus its all-trophies list (roadmap, 100% checklist,
18 card sets). No source swap was needed: all eight were read, [5] and [6] as
tracker listings (list scope), the rest as full pages or key sections.

## The 24 candidates

1. Plain Ledger — one long table, guide under each row. Safe, dull.
2. Neon Vice — dark neon single column, big type, guide cards.
3. Sunledger Atlas — light paper ledger, three panes: stage-grouped checklist,
   open guide, numbered sources rail; audit strip under the header.
4. Map First — a stylised Leonida map as home, trophies pinned to regions.
5. Chapter Book — one chapter per page, trophies inside their chapter.
6. Card Wall — 55 collectible-card tiles, flip for the guide.
7. Spreadsheet Pro — dense grid, sortable columns, guide in a drawer.
8. Timeline River — a horizontal point-of-no-return timeline as the home view.
9. Missable Siren — missables-first design; everything sorted by risk.
10. Two-Character Split — Jason column vs Lucia column, shared middle.
11. Radio Dial — views as radio stations; playful, weak on scanning.
12. Minimal Ink — text-only, fastest load, almost no visual identity.
13. Darkroom — dark theme, red audit accents, photo-negative panels.
14. Boarding Pass — travel-document styling, stamps for earned trophies.
15. Case File — detective dossier per trophy, evidence tags for citations.
16. Atlas Tabs — tabbed views only (Tracker / Roadmap / Matrix / Export).
17. Sunledger Compact — candidate 3 squeezed to two panes, rail as a view.
18. Ledger Noir — candidate 3's structure in dark ink-and-gold.
19. Checklist Maximal — every trophy expanded at once, no detail panel.
20. Roadmap Poster — one giant roadmap poster, tracker secondary.
21. Source Hawk — source matrix as the home view, tracker secondary.
22. Phone Book — mimics the in-game phone; charming, cramped on desktop.
23. Dual Ledger — tracker left, roadmap right, guide below the fold.
24. Quiet Paper — candidate 3 with the audit strip and rails removed.

## Pairwise rounds (winner first)

Round of 24: 3 beat 1 (same data, far better glanceability, crit. 4);
7 beat 12 (drawer guide beats bare text on crit. 1); 17 beat 24 (audit strip
and rails are the leaders' proven shape, crit. 3); 18 beat 2 (structure beats
neon gloss, crit. 4); 9 beat 21 (risk order serves crit. 2 better than matrix
first); 5 beat 22 (desktop width wins, crit. 4); 8 beat 20 (timeline plus
tracker beats poster, crit. 1); 15 beat 6 (evidence tags make citations
visible, crit. 3); 13 beat 10 (one list beats a split list, crit. 1);
16 beat 4 (no invented geography, crit. 2); 23 beat 11 (serious layout beats
the radio gag, crit. 4); 14 beat 19 (collapsed rows scan faster, crit. 1).

Quarter-finals: 3 beat 7 (stage grouping in play order beats sort columns,
crit. 2); 17 beat 18 (light paper reads smaller when shrunk, crit. 4);
9 beat 5 (missable honesty up front, crit. 2); 15 beat 8 (per-trophy case
files beat a lone timeline, crit. 3); 13 beat 16 (tabs alone hide the guide,
crit. 1); 23 beat 14 (dual view beats the gimmick, crit. 4).

Semi-finals: 3 beat 17 (the persistent sources rail is worth its width —
citations stay visible in every screenshot, crit. 3); 15 beat 9 (case-file
detail generalises beyond missables, crit. 1); 3 beat 23 (one open guide
beside the list beats guide-below-the-fold, crit. 1).

Final: 3 vs 15 vs 13. 15's evidence-tag language was folded into 3's detail
panel (citation chips per step, disagreement and missable boxes styled as
file notes). 13's dark contrast lost to paper on shrink-legibility.

## Winner

**Sunledger Atlas** — a light paper-ledger three-pane tracker: stage-grouped
checklist with counters and confidence marks on the rows, the open trophy's
fully cited guide in the middle, a persistent numbered source register on the
right, a computed audit strip and a red missable alert under the header, and
Roadmap / Missables / Source matrix / Export views one click away. Vanilla JS,
one stylesheet, one data JSON: the smallest first load in the final.
